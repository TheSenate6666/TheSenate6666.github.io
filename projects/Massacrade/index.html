Massacrade

Design Overview

Breakdown

Massacrade is a first-person parkour melee slasher set in an elegant city where you take control of Evelyn, a brutal rogue assassin on the run, using her acrobatic movement and combat expertise to escape. The game combines fluid parkour mechanics with intense melee combat, creating a unique blend of traversal and combat gameplay that demands both precision and creativity from the player.

The core loop revolves around navigating through verticality-rich environments, engaging enemies with visceral combat encounters, and escaping through a combination of parkour skills and tactical positioning. Every movement feels weighty and deliberate, while combat feedback systems reinforce the impact of each strike and evasion.

Technical Details

Engine: Unreal Engine 5.7
Primary Language: Blueprint (with C++ for core systems)
Key Systems: Gameplay Ability System, Character Controller, Camera Mode Stack
Team Size: Core team of 5, 3 Sound engineers, 2 Directors
Type: 6th Semester Project
Production Period: 4 months

Game Design

The design philosophy behind Massacrade centers on creating a seamless experience where movement and combat feel like extensions of each other. Rather than separating traversal from encounters, we integrated them into a unified system where the player's parkour skills directly influence their combat capabilities and escape options.

Player Feedback: Every action receives immediate visual, audio, and haptic feedback. Successful parkour movements trigger satisfying animations and sound design, while combat strikes produce impactful visuals and screen shake. Failed movements result in clear, predictable consequences that teach the player without frustration.

Level Design: Our levels are designed with verticality and flow in mind. Multiple pathways encourage player experimentation, and environmental design naturally teaches mechanics through exploration. Sight lines guide players toward objectives while allowing skilled players to discover shortcuts and alternative routes.

Game Feel: Polish was a critical focus throughout development. Animations blend smoothly, the camera remains responsive to player input, and the overall feel communicates player agency at all times. We prioritized making the game feel responsive rather than weighty, encouraging aggressive, flowing play.

Technical Design

The technical foundation of Massacrade is built on the Gameplay Ability System, which provides a scalable framework for managing abilities, effects, and gameplay interactions. This approach allowed us to rapidly prototype and iterate on mechanics while maintaining clean, data-driven design.

Gameplay Ability System (GAS): All character abilities—from basic movement to combat attacks—are implemented as GAS abilities. This unified system enables easy replication for networked play, consistent cooldown management, and modular ability design that scales with the project complexity.

Camera System: We developed a sophisticated camera mode stack that dynamically switches between multiple camera configurations based on character state. The camera responds to movement intensity, combat encounters, and player input, always prioritizing visibility and communicating game state through positioning and animation.

Movement Mechanics: The parkour system includes running, jumping, wall running, climbing, vaulting, and sliding. Each ability is carefully tuned for responsiveness, with input buffering to anticipate player actions and create the feeling of tight, precise controls even in complex movement chains.

Combat Integration: Combat abilities are seamlessly integrated with movement through a state machine that determines valid attack windows, recovery times, and momentum preservation. Players can chain attacks into movement or vice versa, creating dynamic, fluid encounters.

Key Learnings

GAS Mastery: Deep understanding of how to leverage the Gameplay Ability System for complex ability interactions. We learned to structure abilities for optimal performance and maintainability, essential lessons for scalable game architecture.

Feedback Design: The critical importance of multi-layered feedback (visual, audio, haptic) in creating satisfying game feel. Each mechanic requires at least three feedback channels to feel complete and responsive.

Team Collaboration: Working within a team structure of 10+ people taught us the importance of clear communication, modular design, and maintaining consistent vision across different specialties and departments.

Level Design Principles: How to teach complex mechanics through environmental design, player affordances, and sight lines rather than relying on explicit tutorials. Good level design is invisible—it guides without instructing.

Iterative Polish: The importance of continuous playtesting and refinement in achieving professional-level game feel. Small tweaks to animation timing, camera response, and feedback systems compound into dramatically improved player experience.
