Design Overview

Breakdown

Massacrade is a first-person parkour melee slasher set in an elegant city where you take control of Evelyn, a brutal rogue assassin on the run, using her acrobatic movement and combat expertise to escape. The game combines fluid parkour mechanics with intense melee combat, creating a blend of traversal and combat.

The core loop revolves around navigating through a verticality-rich city in the first linear section, where the player learns the mechanics of the game. In the second area, the player must use their newly learned skill to defeat multiple waves of enemies to escape the police forces.

Game Design

The design philosophy behind Massacrade centers on creating a seamless parkour movement and combing it with fast paced melee combat. The goal was to keep the player inside a constantly moving Flowstate while navigating a rich art-deco city enviorement.

Player Controller:My goal was to make the Player feel in control of their movement at any given moment during gameplay. Added to that i tried to make the movement feel really fast-paced, while interacting with the enviorement, so that the players really feels imersed and like a part of the game wolrd. The Player should be rewarded for choosing challenging parkour parts or thinking creatively to traverse the enviorement. To enfore this i decided that every action not only increases the players maximum run speed and acceleration after performance but to also give the player the option to maintain a lot of the momentum but chaining movement actions together.

Combat System: Originally the combat system of the game was based on a weakspot system that is still in place for 2 out of the 4 Enemies. The original idea was that the players needs to hit one or multiple weakspots that were randomized out of predetermend locations. After Playtesting i realized that the original goal of specific slices was a in big contrast to my main design goal of keeping the player moving constantly. Combat quickly became awkward because Players needed to run in circles multiple times to kill enemies.

Camera System:I tried mimicing Mirror's Edge "Gopro" Prarkour Style by making the camera reactive to everything the player does. Key Design pillars were the minimization of motion sickniss while still maximizing immersive fieldof view. On the technical side my camera system works simular to the one build inside of the Unreal Engine Lyra Starting game demo. The Player has diffrent Camera Modes and when a action is performed it gets pushed onto a Camerastack.I tried to focus on having custom transitions for all diffrent actions and making a lot of diffrent camera shakes. Some actions also required multiple camera shakes based on fall height.

Game Feel and Playtesting: Polish was a critical focus throughout development. Animations blend smoothly, the camera remains responsive to player input, and the overall feel communicates player agency at all times. We prioritized making the game feel responsive rather than weighty, encouraging aggressive, flowing play.

Technical Design

The technical foundation of Massacrade is built on the Gameplay Ability System, which provides a scalable framework for managing abilities, effects, and gameplay interactions. This approach allowed us to rapidly prototype and iterate on mechanics while maintaining clean, data-driven design.

Gameplay Ability System (GAS): All character abilities—from basic movement to combat attacks—are implemented as GAS abilities. This unified system enables easy replication for networked play, consistent cooldown management, and modular ability design that scales with the project complexity.

Camera System: We developed a sophisticated camera mode stack that dynamically switches between multiple camera configurations based on character state. The camera responds to movement intensity, combat encounters, and player input, always prioritizing visibility and communicating game state through positioning and animation.

Movement Mechanics: The parkour system includes running, jumping, wall running, climbing, vaulting, and sliding. Each ability is carefully tuned for responsiveness, with input buffering to anticipate player actions and create the feeling of tight, precise controls even in complex movement chains.

Combat and Body Dismemberment Integration: Combat abilities are seamlessly integrated with movement through a state machine that determines valid attack windows, recovery times, and momentum preservation. Players can chain attacks into movement or vice versa, creating dynamic, fluid encounters.

Key Learnings

GAS Mastery: Deep understanding of how to leverage the Gameplay Ability System for complex ability interactions. We learned to structure abilities for optimal performance and maintainability, essential lessons for scalable game architecture.

Feedback Design: The critical importance of multi-layered feedback (visual, audio, haptic) in creating satisfying game feel. Each mechanic requires at least three feedback channels to feel complete and responsive.

Team Collaboration: Working within a team structure of 10+ people taught us the importance of clear communication, modular design, and maintaining consistent vision across different specialties and departments.

Level Design Principles: How to teach complex mechanics through environmental design, player affordances, and sight lines rather than relying on explicit tutorials. Good level design is invisible—it guides without instructing.

Iterative Polish: The importance of continuous playtesting and refinement in achieving professional-level game feel. Small tweaks to animation timing, camera response, and feedback systems compound into dramatically improved player experience.
