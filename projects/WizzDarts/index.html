WizzDarts

Design Overview

Breakdown

WizzDarts is a chaotic 2 player local versus Darts game with roguelike aspects. The Players can use their wizard skills to sabotage their friends with magical items and special darts in the most chaotical game of Darts ever!

The core loop revolves around both players taking turns throwing their darts at the board with the help of the nintendo switch joycons as their darts using motiong controls. After each round both player choose character upgrades and modifiers aswell as special darts to enhance their next round. The goal is to be quick: Since both player choose from the same item pool, they can "steal" the strongest upgrade from the other player or pick specific items that work well with their build. After Each round, a modifier gets added to the dartboard to make the game more chaotic! After a custom amount of rounds the player with the higher score wins!

Game Design

The design philosophy behind Wizzdarts focuses on creating a chaotic local versus party gameexperience, where skills matters while still keeping the barrier of entry as low as possible and minimazing skill gabs through randimazition of events. The goal is to provide a fun expirience for all players with high replay value: No game should be the same as the last!

Player Controller: My goal was to create a player controller that feels good to use while also mimicing the throw of dart player in real life. Since the success of this game would be tied to the fun factor of the throwing mechanic, i decided to spend a lot of production time on polishing this mechnaic. To make the player controller as polished as possible i tried to fit Steven Swink's seven Principles of Game Feel described in his book "Game feel": 1. Predictable Result: When a player throws a dart, it should land exactly where the player would exspect the dart to land. To achieve this, i split up the input in two phases, more on this later. 2. Instantaneous Response: The Player feels the response of their input immediatly. I achieved this by adding vibration and immediate audio and visual cues. 3. Easy but deep: The Game takes minutes to learn but lifetime to master. I tried to make the game easy to learn and be able to consistently throw in the wanted direction, however it is hard to master, if the player wants the dart to land exactly where the aim cursos is by throwing with the exact wanted force. 4. Appealing Response: The Sensation of control is aesthetically appealing and compelling, separate from context. To implement this principle, i tried to create a kitbash and create diffrent visual effects, shaders and audio cues. 5.Organic motion: Controlling the avatar creates appealing arcs of motion. To fullfill this principle, i decided to look at pro darts players to see their arc when throwing a dart. As a big fan of professional darts and a player myself, i know i wanted to reward the player for a clean throwing arc, while also giving a rewarding feedback when the player managed to throw a perfect throw. To get this arc as satisfying as possible, i playtested this game a lot with new and returning players to dinf a good median. 6.Harmony: Each Element of a games feel supports a single, cohesive perception of a unique physical reality of the player. This principle was achieved by incentivizing the player to change up their throw based on the items, darts or board modifiers active. 7.Novelty:  Though the result of the input is predictable, there is enough subtlety. While goof players should be able to score consistenly preceise throws, there must be a added randomness inside the throwing mechanic and by changing up the board via modfiers so the game stays fun and challenging at all times. and playstyle to hit the exact aim spot the player must follow this This feature took a lot of iteration, while originally starting the game as a pc balatro-like with the mouse as input method, i decided to switch to the nintendo switch as a platform. The technical possibilities of the joycons allowed for a better gamefeel thanks to motion controls. After a lot of iteration and trying out a lot of diffrent sensor methods, i decided on a two phase input method: First the player can choose a general desired direction of the dart by tilting and moving the joycon in diffrent directions. When the player decided on a location they could save the location by pressing the trigger button. After that the player enters the aiming phase. While holding the trigegr down the player could now throw the dart by mimicing the throw of a real dart and release the dart when letting go of the trigger. The throw would then be based around the previous selected location with the strength deciding the Y-Axis Location and the Rotation deciding the X-Axis Location based around the previous assigned target location. WHen performing a "perfect" throw the dart would land exactly on the target symbol. Reason for this two phase method was to make the game accessible for players of all skill levels. During the firts playtest i noticed that without the aiming phase players struggeld a lot to guess a general direction of the dart trajectory. This problem got worse when board modifiers were added, that punishes players for throwing in diffrent zones of the board. The Introduction of the aiming phase solved this problem in a nice way. this issue.

Technical Design

The technical foundation of Massacrade is built on the Gameplay Ability System, which provides a scalable framework for managing abilities, effects, and gameplay interactions. This approach allowed us to rapidly prototype and iterate on mechanics while maintaining clean, data-driven design.

Gameplay Ability System (GAS): All character abilities—from basic movement to combat attacks—are implemented as GAS abilities. This unified system enables easy replication for networked play, consistent cooldown management, and modular ability design that scales with the project complexity.

Camera System: We developed a sophisticated camera mode stack that dynamically switches between multiple camera configurations based on character state. The camera responds to movement intensity, combat encounters, and player input, always prioritizing visibility and communicating game state through positioning and animation.

Movement Mechanics: The parkour system includes running, jumping, wall running, climbing, vaulting, and sliding. Each ability is carefully tuned for responsiveness, with input buffering to anticipate player actions and create the feeling of tight, precise controls even in complex movement chains.

Combat Integration: Combat abilities are seamlessly integrated with movement through a state machine that determines valid attack windows, recovery times, and momentum preservation. Players can chain attacks into movement or vice versa, creating dynamic, fluid encounters.

Key Learnings

GAS Mastery: Deep understanding of how to leverage the Gameplay Ability System for complex ability interactions. We learned to structure abilities for optimal performance and maintainability, essential lessons for scalable game architecture.

Feedback Design: The critical importance of multi-layered feedback (visual, audio, haptic) in creating satisfying game feel. Each mechanic requires at least three feedback channels to feel complete and responsive.

Team Collaboration: Working within a team structure of 10+ people taught us the importance of clear communication, modular design, and maintaining consistent vision across different specialties and departments.

Level Design Principles: How to teach complex mechanics through environmental design, player affordances, and sight lines rather than relying on explicit tutorials. Good level design is invisible—it guides without instructing.

Iterative Polish: The importance of continuous playtesting and refinement in achieving professional-level game feel. Small tweaks to animation timing, camera response, and feedback systems compound into dramatically improved player experience.
